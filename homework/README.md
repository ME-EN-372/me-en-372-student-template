# Homework

Thirteen problem sets, `hw01/` through `hw13/`, 100 points each. One folder per
set, one file per problem, plus a `figures/` folder for your sketches.

    homework/
      hw01/
        problem-1.md
        problem-2.md
        figures/
          hw01-p1-fbd.jpg

Start from [`TEMPLATE-solution.md`](TEMPLATE-solution.md) every time. Its
headings are the BYU ME structured problem-solving format, which is what the
exams are graded against as well.

**Due Fridays at 5:00 pm Mountain Time**, submitted on Canvas. **Photographs of
hand work are fine.** Legible beats typeset; nobody is grading your LaTeX.

## You grade your own work

Homework is formative. Its job is to build the problem-solving habit before it
is certified on the exams and the project, so you grade your own process against
a released key.

1. Submit by the Friday 5:00 pm deadline. The solution guide goes up on Canvas
   only after the deadline.
2. Grade your own submission against the per-problem checklists in that guide,
   in a different color of ink or as PDF annotations, so the grading marks are
   distinguishable from the original work.
3. Award partial credit exactly as the checklist rows describe. The rows are
   independent: a correct final number with no visible setup earns the result
   row and not the setup rows.
4. Classify your errors. Each problem's "Common errors" block names the mistakes
   that are most likely, such as diameter versus radius or millimetres versus
   metres. Note in the margin which class yours fell into. Rounding differences
   are free; a factor of 2, 4, or a million is worth hunting down and naming.
5. Total the score out of 100 and enter it on Canvas as directed.
6. **If you score below 70**, rework the missed problems and bring the marked-up
   sheet to office hours or the Thursday lab. Seventy is the on-track line, not
   a punishment line.

A sample of submissions is re-graded each week so the keys stay calibrated
across both sections. The grade you most need to be accurate is your own.
